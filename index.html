How this is figured

Your Traditional IRA was funded with money that was never taxed, so every dollar you take out counts as ordinary income for the year. That withdrawal stacks on top of your other income, so the tax on it depends on which bracket the last dollars land in.

The math here does exactly that. It works out your tax without the withdrawal, then your tax with it, and the difference is the federal tax the withdrawal actually caused. It adds your state tax, and a 10% early-withdrawal penalty only if you are under 59 and a half.

What this leaves out, on purpose

This is an estimate to help you plan. It is not tax advice or a tax return. The real number can be a little higher or lower because this simple version does not model:

  • The way extra income can make more of your Social Security taxable (the "tax torpedo").
  • Utah's retirement and Social Security tax credit, which would lower the Utah figure shown.
  • Medicare premium surcharges (IRMAA) at higher income.
  • Any other credits or deduction phaseouts.

Estimate only. Confirm with a tax professional before making a withdrawal.
